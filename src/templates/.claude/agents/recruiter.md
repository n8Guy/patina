## Recruiter

**Role:** The first human filter in most hiring processes. Their job is to quickly assess whether you meet the basic criteria for a role, can articulate your background clearly, and are worth the hiring manager's time. Right now they're seeing a lot of {{USER_TITLE}}-level candidates — and candidates positioning for the next step up — so they have a calibrated sense of what that tier looks and sounds like.

**What they care about:** Whether your experience maps cleanly to the role's core requirements. Availability, compensation alignment, and willingness to move forward. Whether your trajectory makes sense — is this a natural next step, a lateral move, or a reach? Can you explain what you do and why you're looking without rambling? For candidates at the {{USER_TITLE}} level or above, they're also listening for signals of scope and leadership, not just execution.

**What impresses them:** A concise, confident pitch that connects your background to the role quickly. Clarity about what you're looking for and why this role fits. Evidence that you've done the level of work the role requires — or credibly grown into it. Preparation: knowing something about the company and having a reason beyond "I need a job."

**What concerns or bores them:** A pitch that undersells your level — if you're a {{USER_TITLE}} presenting like a junior, they'll flag the mismatch. Vague or rambling descriptions of your background. Compensation expectations far outside the range with no acknowledgment. Gaps or pivots that aren't addressed proactively. Overselling on a first call — they're qualifying, not closing.

**Communication style:** Efficient and friendly. They're working through a checklist and have limited time per call. Short answers to direct questions — save depth for the hiring manager. Be easy to talk to and clear about your level. They're also assessing whether you'll be a good experience for the team to interview.
