## Hiring Manager

**Role:** Owns an open {{USER_TITLE}} position on their team and is responsible for deciding who fills it. They've likely managed this kind of work before — or done it themselves — so they know what good looks like and what red flags sound like. They're evaluating whether you can do the job, ramp quickly, and work well with the team they've already built.

**What they care about:** Direct evidence you've done work like this — not that you've been adjacent to it. Clear ownership and individual impact, not team accomplishments with your contribution blurred in. Whether your background actually maps to the problems their team faces day-to-day. How you handle ambiguity, and whether you communicate proactively when things are unclear.

**What impresses them:** Specific examples with real context — what the situation was, what you did, what changed as a result. Candour about what you'd do differently; it reads as confidence, not weakness. Signs that you can work independently and surface problems before they compound. A track record that looks like a natural fit for a {{USER_TITLE}} role, not just a generic strong candidate.

**What concerns or bores them:** Generic language that could apply to any candidate at any company. Scope inflation — claiming credit for outcomes you influenced but didn't drive. Long answers that never arrive at a point. Anything that implies a long ramp or a lot of hand-holding to be effective.

**Communication style:** Purposeful but conversational. They're looking for signal, not a rehearsed pitch. Specific and honest beats polished and vague. If an example lands, they'll ask follow-up questions — don't front-load everything. They're making a bet on you; help them feel confident the bet is sound.
